01 National Youth Media Team Critical ▾
Expert Analysis
This is the most important structural reform in the entire document. Without a functioning youth media team representing all three language mediums (Sinhala, Tamil, English), every other recommendation remains a vision on paper. Globally, scout associations like BSA (USA) and Scouts UK operate dedicated youth communications units with clear mandates and mentorship pipelines. SLSA must do the same.
Global benchmark: Scouts Australia's National Youth Council has a dedicated media sub-team with members aged 14–25. They produce 80% of social media content. Result: 3× engagement increase in 2 years.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Draft a formal Terms of Reference (ToR) for the National Youth Media Team with roles: Team Lead, Sinhala Editor, Tamil Editor, English Editor, Videographer, Graphic Designer.
  2. Conduct an island-wide open call for scouts (Cub → Rover) with media skills — photography, video editing, graphic design, copywriting — across all districts.
  3. Shortlist 10–15 members ensuring representation from all 3 language mediums and all provinces.
  4. Assign a senior Scout Leader as a mentor/advisor with a monthly briefing schedule.
  5. Create a shared Google Workspace or Microsoft Teams environment for collaboration.
  6. Hold a 2-day residential training camp combining media skills + scouting values before official launch.
02 Official YouTube Channel Critical ▾
Expert Analysis
YouTube is the second-most visited website in Sri Lanka, and YouTube Shorts is projected to dominate brand promotion in Sri Lanka through 2026. A scout songs, crafts, and yells channel is an excellent entry point because this content is timeless, searchable, and family-friendly. AI-assisted production (auto-captioning, translation, background removal) can significantly reduce production costs.
Global benchmark: World Scouting's official YouTube has 185K+ subscribers with content in 12 languages. Their "Scout Skills" playlist alone has 2M+ views — proof that evergreen skills content drives traffic.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Register the channel as "Sri Lanka Scouts Official" — secure the handle immediately to prevent squatting.
  2. Create 3 playlists to start: Scout Songs, Scout Skills & Crafts, Scout Yells & Cheers.
  3. Film the first 5 videos with a volunteer film crew at a district event — a song performed by cubs, a knot-tying tutorial, a campfire yell.
  4. Use free AI tools (CapCut, DaVinci Resolve, auto-captions) to polish videos before upload.
  5. Upload consistently: 1 video per week minimum. Use Shorts (under 60 seconds) for quick skill demos.
  6. Add Sinhala, Tamil, and English auto-subtitles for all videos — YouTube provides this free.
03 Social Media Reach — Reels & Short Video High ▾
Expert Analysis
Short-form video is not optional — it is the dominant content format for Sri Lankan youth in 2026. Research shows TikTok has the highest engagement rate globally at 3.73%, far ahead of other platforms. Instagram Reels reach non-followers, making them the best tool for growth. SLSA should target 3–5 Reels per week, not per month.
Global benchmark: Scouts New Zealand grew from 8K to 47K Instagram followers in 18 months by posting daily Reels of camp activities, badge challenges, and behind-the-scenes scout life content.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Establish a content calendar with 4 content pillars: Scout Skills, Behind the Scenes, Scout Stories (member spotlight), Event Highlights.
  2. Film Reels at every district and national event — assign a youth media member to each event specifically for Reels capture.
  3. Post 3 Reels per week on both Facebook and Instagram. Use the same vertical video across both platforms.
  4. Add Sinhala and Tamil captions using free tools like CapCut to maximize local reach.
  5. Use trending audio in Reels — check Instagram's audio trending section weekly and apply to scout content.
04 Media Quality Standards High ▾
Expert Analysis
Inconsistent visual branding destroys credibility. Every image or video shared without proper logos and watermarks makes SLSA look amateur. A single, downloadable "Brand Kit" must be created and distributed to all district media reps. This is standard practice in all credible scouting organizations and aligns with WOSM's 2024 refreshed brand guidelines.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Create a free SLSA Canva Team account — upload official logos, color codes (#), and approved fonts as brand assets accessible to all districts.
  2. Design 3–5 standard post templates: Event Announcement, Achievement Highlight, Quote Card, Event Recap.
  3. Publish a 1-page "SLSA Social Media Standards" PDF — watermark placement, logo size rules, image resolution minimum (1080×1080px for posts, 1080×1920px for Stories/Reels).
  4. Mandate that all official accounts use these templates before posting.
  5. Conduct a quarterly "content audit" — review recent posts from all district accounts for standards compliance.
05 Production Quality — District Resources High ▾
Expert Analysis
Districts have untapped talent — scouts with DSLR cameras, video editing software, and even drone licenses. A resource mapping exercise will reveal what already exists. The challenge is coordination, not resources. A "District Media Resource Registry" connecting skilled scouts to national-level projects will transform production quality without additional cost.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Send a skills survey to all district commissioners — ask scouts to self-identify as: photographer, videographer, video editor, graphic designer, drone operator.
  2. Create a shared national registry (Google Sheet or Notion) listing available equipment and skills by district.
  3. Organize a "Media Skills Day" — a half-day workshop at each district event where scouts teach each other photography/video basics.
  4. Nominate a "District Media Officer" in each district to coordinate production and maintain equipment standards.
06 WOSM Logo Usage — URGENT Critical ▾
Expert Analysis
This is time-sensitive. WOSM officially refreshed its brand and visual identity in May 2024 — a completely redesigned World Scout Emblem with updated logo and wordmark was approved by the World Scout Committee. Many SLSA districts are still using the old logo, which is now outdated. WOSM also provides a free "Brand Centre" with downloadable assets at learn.scout.org. Immediate action is needed.
WOSM Update: The new World Scouting brand was approved in May 2024 with a "bold, modern, youthful, and digital" identity specifically designed for social media use. SLSA must communicate this to all districts immediately.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Download the updated WOSM brand assets from the official WOSM Brand Centre: learn.scout.org → "World Scouting Brand Centre".
  2. Issue an official circular from the Chief Commissioner to all districts — attach the updated logo files in PNG, SVG, and JPG formats.
  3. Conduct a 1-hour online awareness webinar specifically on logo usage rules — record it and share via WhatsApp and email.
  4. Remove all outdated logos from official digital accounts within 30 days of the circular.
  5. Add a "Logo Compliance Check" to the annual district review process.
07 SLSA Logo Usage Guidelines High ▾
Expert Analysis
Without written guidelines, scouts will continue using the SLSA logo on unofficial merchandise, personal posts, and non-approved materials — diluting brand identity. A simple, illustrated 2-page guideline document is sufficient. Model it after WOSM's Brand Manual available on the Scout Learning Zone.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Draft a 2-page "SLSA Logo Usage Guide" covering: approved colors, minimum sizes, clear space rules, and prohibited uses (stretching, recoloring, adding effects).
  2. Translate into all three languages — Sinhala, Tamil, English.
  3. Distribute via email, WhatsApp, and pin on the official SLSA website and social media profiles.
  4. Create a simple "Do and Don't" visual graphic — easy for young scouts to understand.
08 WhatsApp Channel Critical ▾
Expert Analysis
WhatsApp is used by the vast majority of Sri Lankan youth and their parents. A WhatsApp Channel (one-way broadcast, not a group) is ideal for SLSA — it allows instant, direct communication to thousands of followers without spam concerns. This is fundamentally different from WhatsApp groups and does not expose member phone numbers. Research confirms Facebook, WhatsApp, and YouTube are the top 3 platforms among Sri Lankan youth.
Sri Lanka context: Facebook dominates with ~7 million users, but WhatsApp is used for daily communication — especially for school-aged youth coordinating within scout groups. A Channel reaches them where they already are.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Create the official "SLSA Updates" WhatsApp Channel from the national HQ account.
  2. Share the Channel link in all existing WhatsApp scout groups — district groups, leader groups, parent groups.
  3. Post on all social media platforms asking members to subscribe: "Follow our WhatsApp Channel for instant event updates."
  4. Assign a dedicated Channel Manager (from the youth media team) to post updates 3–5 times per week.
  5. Content types: Event reminders, registration deadlines, weather updates for camps, quick photo highlights, badge achievement announcements.
  6. Also create district-level WhatsApp Channels linked to the national one for local news.
09 Scout Profile — The Scarf Identity High ▾
Expert Analysis
The scout scarf (necker) is one of the most recognizable symbols of scouting worldwide. When scouts appear in public without it, a major visibility opportunity is missed. This is not about discipline — it is about pride and identity. The solution is positive reinforcement and clear, practical guidance on when the scarf must be worn, using cheerful, youth-friendly communication rather than top-down instructions.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Issue a simple "When to wear your scarf" illustrated guide — include: all official scout events, public service activities, travel to/from events, national days.
  2. Start a social media campaign: #ProudToBeAScout — encourage scouts to post photos in full uniform including scarf, tagging SLSA.
  3. Recognize and spotlight scouts who consistently maintain proper uniform on the SLSA social media pages.
  4. At every award ceremony, explicitly acknowledge correct uniform wearing as part of scout excellence.
  5. Distribute a pocket-card reminder to all scouts at the start of each scouting year listing uniform requirements.
10 Scout Profile — Public Awareness Events High ▾
Expert Analysis
Scout Service Month and Scout Week are globally recognized scouting milestones that SLSA consistently underutilizes. The strategy proposed — social media content + Chief Commissioner interviews + community feedback — is correct and comprehensive. The missing ingredient is a centralized, coordinated content calendar pushed to all districts simultaneously, not ad-hoc posts.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Mark Scout Service Month and Scout Week on a national SLSA content calendar shared with all districts 60 days in advance.
  2. Film a 3–5 minute video interview with the Chief Commissioner and ADC Programme to be released on the first day of Scout Week.
  3. Create a "30 Days of Scouting Impact" Instagram/Facebook campaign during Scout Service Month — one post daily highlighting a different service project.
  4. Collect short video testimonials from community beneficiaries of scout projects — these are the most powerful content type for public awareness.
  5. Send a press kit to Sri Lankan national media (Rupavahini, ITN, Hiru, Daily News) before Scout Week with key facts and interview requests.
  6. Collaborate with the Ministry of Education to issue a school circular recognizing Scout Week activities.
11 Social Media Trends — Modern Content High ▾
Expert Analysis
Global scouting content that goes viral shares common traits: authentic scout moments (not staged), outdoor adventure footage, skill demonstrations, and day-in-the-life formats. Camp and hike content performs exceptionally well because it showcases what non-scouts wish they could do. The "adventure and skill" identity of scouting is a natural fit for trending content formats — SLSA simply needs to capture and package it correctly.
Global trend: "Day in the life at scout camp" format videos consistently receive 5–10× more engagement than official announcement posts. Authenticity beats production value every time for youth audiences.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. At every camp and hike, assign a "Content Scout" responsible for capturing candid, authentic moments — not just posed group photos.
  2. Create a "Scout Challenge" series — short Reels where scouts demonstrate a skill (fire lighting, first aid, tent pitching) and challenge others to try.
  3. Use trending audio from Instagram/TikTok — check the "Trending" audio section weekly and overlay camp/hike footage.
  4. Post "Before/After" transformation Reels — "Before scout training vs. After" — these are highly shareable.
  5. Follow global scout accounts (World Scouting, Scouts UK, BSA, Scouts Australia) and adapt their trending content formats to a Sri Lankan context.
12 Youth Media Appointments High ▾
Expert Analysis
Youth involvement in decision-making is a core pillar of WOSM's Strategy for Scouting. WOSM explicitly emphasizes youth leadership and youth involvement across all levels. Appointing youth to formal roles like Assistant District Commissioner (ADC) for Media creates accountability, mentorship opportunities, and real leadership development — which is ultimately what scouting is about.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Create a formal "ADC Media (Youth)" designation within the district commissioner structure — open only to scouts aged 18–25.
  2. Advertise these positions openly at district level with clear selection criteria: media skills + scouting record + language proficiency.
  3. Pair each ADC Media Youth with an experienced adult ADC Media as a mentor for the first year.
  4. Include youth ADC Media members in all national media committee meetings with full voting rights.
  5. Recognize these appointments publicly via social media and at annual award ceremonies.
13 Youth Representation at District Meetings Medium ▾
Expert Analysis
Allowing 2 sub-committee youth representatives to attend ADC Media meetings is a practical, low-cost reform with high impact on communication and alignment. District-level challenges are invisible to the national sub-committee unless there is a structured feedback channel. This creates one.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Formally amend the ADC Media meeting agenda to include a standing "Youth Report" agenda item (10 minutes) presented by the 2 youth reps.
  2. Rotate the 2 youth representative positions every 6 months to expose more youth to district-level governance.
  3. Have youth reps submit a 1-page written summary after each meeting to the national sub-committee chair.
  4. Create a shared WhatsApp group connecting all district youth media reps with the national sub-committee for ongoing communication.
14 Commemorative Social Media Content High ▾
Expert Analysis
Scarf Day, F.G. Stevenson Day, Nature Day, World Scout Day, Thinking Day — all of these represent zero-cost content opportunities that SLSA is missing. A simple annual commemorative content calendar, pre-scheduled 3 months in advance, would ensure no important date is missed. This builds brand identity and gives the audience regular reasons to engage.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Create a comprehensive "SLSA Annual Commemorative Content Calendar" listing every relevant day with: post theme, suggested visual concept, and platform.
  2. Pre-design template posts for each commemorative day — so they can be posted with minimal last-minute effort.
  3. For major dates (World Scout Day, Scarf Day), prepare 3 content pieces: 1 static post, 1 Reel, 1 WhatsApp Channel announcement.
  4. Add lesser-known but meaningful days: International Youth Day (12 Aug), World Environment Day (5 Jun, tied to nature activities).
  5. Schedule posts using Meta Business Suite to ensure they go live at optimal times even if the team is offline.
15 TikTok Channel High ▾
Expert Analysis
TikTok has the highest engagement rate of any social platform (3.73% average). Sri Lankan youth heavily use TikTok. Creating a TikTok account is free and scout content — outdoor skills, camp moments, team challenges — is perfectly suited to the platform. The content produced for Reels can be directly repurposed for TikTok with minimal editing. One strategy, multiple platforms.
Strategic note: When India banned TikTok in 2020, users migrated instantly to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Build TikTok but also cross-post to Reels and Shorts simultaneously — this protects against platform risk.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Register @SriLankaScouts on TikTok immediately — verify the account as an official organization.
  2. Repurpose existing Reels content onto TikTok with minor format adjustments (add TikTok-native text, sounds).
  3. Launch with a "7-Day Scout Challenge" — post one TikTok per day for a week showcasing different scout skills to rapidly gain early followers.
  4. Use trending TikTok sounds and challenges as frameworks — overlay scout footage onto viral audio/formats.
  5. Engage with comments actively in the first 90 days — the TikTok algorithm rewards early engagement response.
16 Increasing Social Media Followers Medium ▾
Expert Analysis
Follower count is a lagging indicator — it grows when content quality and consistency are in place. Competitions are a proven growth tactic, but the most sustainable growth comes from consistently posting content that non-scouts want to share. "Tag a friend who needs scouting" posts, shareable skill tips, and emotionally resonant community service content drive organic growth better than giveaways alone.
Step-by-Step Actions
  1. Run a quarterly "Scout Photo Challenge" — members submit their best scout photo with a hashtag. Top 3 are featured on the official page. This is organic follower growth.
  2. Post "Did You Know? Scouting in Sri Lanka" facts 2× per week — these are highly shareable and attract new followers.
  3. Collaborate with popular Sri Lankan youth influencers or content creators to visit a scout camp and post about it — this exposes SLSA to hundreds of thousands of new followers.
  4. Ask current followers to "share this page with one friend who has a child interested in scouting" — direct calls to action work.
  5. Run a referral campaign: "The school group that gets the most new followers to our page wins a trophy at the National Jamboree" — this enlists every scout as a follower recruiter.

Based on 50 years of field experience and current global best practices, these are additional recommendations not covered in the submitted PDF.

A Trilingual Content Strategy — The Missing Gap

The PDF mentions language mediums but never addresses a structured trilingual content strategy. SLSA has scouts across Sinhala, Tamil, and English communities, but nearly all visible social content is in Sinhala only. This alienates Tamil-speaking scouts and their families — a missed recruitment and retention opportunity. Every major post should be translated or at minimum captioned in all 3 languages. This alone could significantly increase membership in Northern and Eastern provinces.

B Scout Alumni Network — Untapped Resource

Sri Lanka has thousands of former scouts, many now in prominent professional roles. None of the 16 recommendations mention engaging scout alumni. A "Once a Scout, Always a Scout" digital alumni campaign — inviting prominent alumni to share their scouting story on social media — would generate massive authentic content and inspire current youth. SLSA should create an Alumni LinkedIn group and feature one "Scout Alumni Spotlight" per month.

C Content Management System — Operational Infrastructure

Currently there is no centralized tool for scheduling, approving, and tracking social media posts across districts. SLSA needs a free tool like Buffer or Meta Business Suite to schedule content in advance, assign approval workflows, and track analytics. Without this, the YouTube channel, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp Channel will become chaotic — managed by different individuals with no coordination.

D Annual SLSA Media Awards

Recognition drives behavior. Create an "SLSA Annual Media Excellence Award" with categories: Best Photo, Best Short Video, Best Social Media Post, Best Event Coverage, Most Improved District Media Team. Award at the Annual Scout Conference. This costs nothing but motivates every district media team to produce high-quality content throughout the year — because they are competing for recognition.

E School & Parent Outreach — Direct to Decision Makers

In Sri Lanka, a child joins scouts because a parent or teacher encouraged it. None of the 16 recommendations address parent and teacher communication. A dedicated "Parents of Scouts" WhatsApp Channel and quarterly newsletter explaining what their children learned — leadership skills, first aid, teamwork — would significantly boost enrollment and retention. Parents who feel informed become the most effective recruiters for new scouts.

F Partnerships with Sri Lankan Media — Earned Media

Earned media (free news coverage) is far more credible than any social media post. SLSA should build formal relationships with Sri Lankan television channels and newspapers. Propose a monthly "Scout Spotlight" segment on a national channel — showcasing a district's community service project. This costs nothing but time and dramatically increases public visibility beyond SLSA's current follower base.

G SLSA Website as a Media Hub

The scout.lk website is currently a repository, not a media hub. Adding a Photo & Video Gallery, a Scout Stories blog, a Press section with downloadable media kits, and an embedded YouTube/social feed would transform it into a destination that generates credibility, supports recruitment, and provides journalists with easy access to SLSA information. A strong website also improves Google search visibility for "scouts Sri Lanka" queries.
